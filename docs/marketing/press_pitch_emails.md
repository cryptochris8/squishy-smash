# Press Pitch Emails — Squishy Smash Book Launch

Drafted 2026-06-04. Four venue-tailored pitches plus follow-up plan + press-kit checklist.

**Story positioning across all pitches:**
- Hook 1 — Local: Spartanburg dad, fiancée and three daughters
- Hook 2 — Cadence: 2 picture books + 1 mobile game in 7 weeks (the speed is news)
- Hook 3 — Craft: $11/book AI-pipeline production vs $3K-20K industry baseline (deeper story for analytical venues)
- Hook 4 — Timing: launched during the squishy toy craze + summer reading season

Lead with whichever hook fits the venue. AI/production detail is the bigger draw for analytical outlets; local-family is the bigger draw for community outlets.

## 4. Studio SC on South Carolina Public Radio

**Best path:** https://www.southcarolinapublicradio.org → "Contact" → "Story Ideas." Studio SC is a long-form interview show; the audience appreciates analytical / craft-of-making content.

**This pitch leads with the AI production story** — it's the angle that fits this venue better than the family angle.

### Subject

```
Story idea — How a Spartanburg dad made a picture book for $11
```

### Body

```
Hello Studio SC team,

I'd like to pitch a story that sits at the intersection of indie publishing, AI, and family life — and I think it'd make a solid long-form interview segment.

I'm Christopher Campbell, a Spartanburg writer and indie game developer. Over the past seven weeks I've published two children's picture books on Amazon and an iPhone game, all part of one series called Squishy Smash. The picture books themselves are gentle, read-aloud-friendly stories for ages 4-8. My fiancée and our three daughters (twin 8s + a 6-year-old) were the test audience. Six books are planned in the series; books three and four are in active production.

The story underneath the books is what I think your listeners would find interesting:

The picture-book illustration industry typically charges $3,000 to $20,000 to illustrate a 32-to-40 page book. That price floor is the reason most indie picture-book authors never make Book 2.

I built an AI image-generation pipeline that produced all 18 painterly spreads of "The Lost Sparkle" for $10.97 in materials cost. Five distinct pipelines failed before the sixth one worked. The journey — and what it implies for the floor of indie children's literature — is the substance of the pitch.

I'm transparent about every part of it: which tools, what failed, what worked, what the genuine limits are (this does not replace human illustrators; it lowers the indie price-floor). I'm also clear-eyed about the moat (the IP itself is the durable thing, not the workflow).

I could do this segment as:
- 12-15 min author interview (book + family + craft)
- Extended segment with another expert (a working illustrator? an AI ethics voice from USC?) for a real conversation about implications

Happy to send a press kit, hi-res author photo, and book copies for the studio.

Thank you for considering.

Chris Campbell
chriscam8@gmail.com
squishysmash.com
```

## Follow-up plan

**Day 0 (send day):** All four pitches go out same day. Same-day coordination creates "local author momentum" — multiple journalists see it landing simultaneously and assume there's interest.

**Day 8 follow-up rules:**
- Print (Herald-Journal): one polite bump email, 4-5 sentences
- Mom blog (Kidding Around Greenville): one DM on Instagram if email is silent
- TV: do NOT follow up by email — instead, tag the morning anchors on X once with the match-cut video. TV producers scout X for booking ideas
- Radio (SC Public Radio): one polite bump email after 14 days; their booking cadence is slower

**Day 21:** If silence everywhere, pivot. Don't keep bumping — switch to:
- A LinkedIn post tagging local journalists by name
- A direct DM to one bylined journalist via X
- An in-person stop by the Herald-Journal building if you're already downtown (acceptable for community papers; never for major dailies)

---

## What NOT to do

- DON'T attach more than 1-2 files to the initial email. Most newsroom inboxes auto-reject attachments over 5 MB. Use links to high-res images instead.
- DON'T copy-paste the same pitch verbatim across all four. Each is venue-tailored above; the openings, hooks, and asks differ deliberately.
- DON'T mention the AI production story to Kidding Around Greenville unless asked. Their audience-reaction risk is asymmetric (high downside, low upside).
- DON'T over-explain Squishy Smash. The bullets sell it; over-explaining reads as nervous.
- DON'T attach the match-cut video to print/radio pitches — only TV. Print editors don't watch videos.
