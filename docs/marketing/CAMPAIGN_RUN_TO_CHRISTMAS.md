# The Run to Christmas — Squishy Smash Book Advertising Campaign

**Sept 1 → Dec 15, 2026 · 105 days · the only quarter a $12.99 picture book actually gets bought.**

*Drafted 2026-08-28. This is the "all out" plan: it supersedes the organic-first budget posture in `marketing/GO_VIRAL_PLAN.md` §11 (game repo) and **wraps** — does not replace — `amazon_ads_launch_plan.md`, which stays the operating manual for the Amazon half.*

---

## 0. The pitch, in one paragraph

We are not buying book sales. At a $4.14 royalty, buying book sales is a losing trade and always will be — no amount of clever targeting fixes $4 unit economics on a cold-traffic product. What we *are* buying, in the only 105 days of the year when gift intent is real, is **four assets that outlive the spend**: a review count that permanently upgrades conversion, a search-term map of who actually buys this book, a creative library that Books 3–6 inherit, and a physical object with a QR code back into the game sitting in a few hundred more households. The book is the highest-margin doorway in the whole Squishy Smash ecosystem and the only one that installs itself permanently in a home. Price the campaign as **launch infrastructure for a six-book series**, not as a book P&L — and if that framing doesn't sit right, run the Scout tier and keep the money.

## 3. Why Q4, specifically

Gifting drives roughly **42% of hardcover children's picture book sales**, and November–December carry close to a quarter of all annual trade book volume — for a premium-color square picture book at $12.99, the gift buyer *is* the customer, and they only exist for about eight weeks. A dollar spent on Nov 30 does not do the same work as a dollar spent in February. This campaign exists because the calendar says so.

The corollary that matters more: **the buyer is usually not the reader.** She is a grandmother, an aunt, a family friend buying for a 5-year-old she sees three times a year. She cannot evaluate the story. She evaluates: does this look lovely, is it age-right, is it safe, will the parents approve. Every piece of creative in §7 is written to *that* person, not to a kid.

**Dates on the wall:**

| Date | What it is | What we do |
|---|---|---|
| Sept 6 (Sun) | Read a Book Day | Read-along video push, free coloring pack |
| Sept 1–30 | Local/blog gift-guide submission window | Pitch every Upstate SC + parenting blog guide (§8) |
| Oct 1 | Meta + Pinterest go live | Creative test winners scale |
| Nov 1 | **Picture Book Month** + Family Literacy Month | The single best earned-media hook of the year for this exact product |
| Nov 26 (Thu) | Thanksgiving | Budgets step up the night before |
| Nov 27 (Fri) | Black Friday | Peak CPCs; hold position, do not discount |
| Nov 30 (Mon) | Cyber Monday | Highest-intent day of the quarter |
| Dec 1–10 | The real peak for print gift buying | Maximum daily budget |
| **Dec 14 (Mon)** | **Last safe order date for Christmas arrival** | Switch all copy to "arrives by Christmas" |
| Dec 15 | Campaign ends | Cut spend ~80% overnight |
| Dec 26 – Jan 5 | Gift-card + new-tablet tail | Small always-on; Kindle edition if it exists by then |

## 4. The three phases

### Phase 1 — LOAD (Sept 1–30) · $540

The month of unglamorous free work. Spend is deliberately trivial.

1. Clear the §2 gate. All of it.
2. Build the creative library (§7) — done: `tools/marketing/build_ad_creative.py` outputs the full set.
3. Run the ARC squad + back-matter QR + Sparkle Letter send. **Target: 5+ reviews by Oct 1, 10 by Nov 1.**
4. Amazon `SS-B2-AUTO-Discovery` at $14/day collecting search terms. No optimization until week 3 — 14-day attribution means early panic-cutting is the classic error.
5. Meta at $4/day as a **creative test only**: 6 images, 1 ad set, cheapest-cost traffic. We are not buying sales in September; we are finding which image stops the thumb before it costs real money.
6. Press: local and blog gift guides close their lists this month. Pitch now or miss the quarter (§8).

### Phase 2 — AIM (Oct 1–31) · $992

1. First Amazon harvest: every search term with 1+ orders becomes an Exact keyword at its converting CPC +10%, negated in the source campaign.
2. Kill the losers: 10+ clicks / 0 orders on search terms; 15–20 clicks / 0 orders on chosen targets.
3. Meta scales to $8/day on the September winner. Two audiences only: **Grandparents (55–70, grandchildren + gift interests)** and **Moms of 3–8s**. Do not build twelve ad sets on a $250/month budget — one creative winner across two audiences.
4. Pinterest opens at $4/day. Pins are the cheapest and longest-lived asset in the plan: a pin published Oct 1 is still delivering in March.
5. Launch `SS-B1-PT-OwnB2` — the defensive cross. A both-books order is **$8.07 of royalty on a single paid click**; the series is the only real ads moat a two-book catalog has.
6. Nov 1 press pitches go out mid-October (Picture Book Month needs 2–3 weeks of lead).

### Phase 3 — FIRE (Nov 1 – Dec 15) · $2,067

1. Budgets step up per the table. Bids +$0.10 across proven Exact and Tier A/B product targets — everyone else raises too, and the auction in December is not the auction in September.
2. **All copy switches to gift framing on Nov 1** and to arrival-urgency on Dec 5.
3. Picture Book Month earned push: the dad-and-three-daughters story is the pitch, the book is the artifact.
4. Local markets and bookstore consignment run hot (§6) — this is where the actual money is made.
5. Dec 14: last-call creative. Dec 15: cut spend 80%, let attribution settle, do not touch anything for a week.

## 5. Channels — structure, targeting, and what each is for

### 5.1 Amazon Sponsored Products — *harvest existing intent*

Run exactly as `amazon_ads_launch_plan.md` specifies: five campaigns, dynamic-bids-down-only, $0.30 floor / $0.90 ceiling, weekly harvest-and-negate loop. This plan changes only the budget ceilings (§1 table) and adds Q4 bid pressure in November.

**What it is for:** capturing people already typing "squishmallow book" and "bedtime stories for 5 year olds." It is the most efficient money in the plan and the least scalable — demand is capped by search volume. It cannot grow the category; it can only take our share of it.

### 5.2 Meta (Instagram + Facebook) — *create intent in the gift buyer*

- **Objective:** Traffic → the Amazon listing (or `squishysmash.com/book`, which redirects — use the redirect so we own the click data). Do **not** run a conversions objective; we have no pixel on Amazon.
- **Attribution reality:** you cannot see Amazon sales from Meta. Set up **Amazon Attribution** tags for the off-Amazon links `[VERIFY KDP eligibility in the Amazon Ads console]`; if unavailable, judge Meta on cost-per-outbound-click and on the Amazon sales lift on days Meta runs. Accept the fuzziness — do not spend $200 of effort building a tracking scheme for a $250/month channel.
- **Audiences (two, that's all):**
  - **A — "Grandma":** 55–70, US, interests = grandchildren / gifts for grandchildren / children's books, broad. Historically the cheapest high-intent picture-book audience in Q4 and the one most likely to buy both books at once.
  - **B — "Mom of a 4–8":** 28–45, US, parents of preschool/early-grade children, interests = children's books, read-aloud, plush/kawaii and Squishmallows-adjacent. *(Interest targeting on a trademark is fine on Meta; the trademark must never appear in our own copy or listing.)*
- **Placements:** Advantage+ placements on. **Creative:** the 4:5 cover ad and the 20s spread video (both built). Run 2 creatives × 2 audiences; kill anything above $0.60 CPC after 1,000 impressions.
- **The one Meta-specific rule:** this is a *scroll-stopping-image* medium and our art is the best thing we own. Lead with the art, never with a text-heavy graphic.

### 5.3 Pinterest — *the compounding sleeper*

The most underrated channel for this exact product. "Bedtime books for 4 year olds," "gifts for granddaughter," and "picture books about friendship" are high-volume evergreen Pinterest searches, CPCs routinely land $0.10–0.40, and — unlike Meta — **a pin keeps delivering for months after the spend stops.** Every dollar here buys an asset, not an impression.

- Publish 15–20 organic pins first (the 2:3 pins are built), then promote the 3 best.
- Boards: Bedtime Books · Gifts for Kids 4–8 · Read-Aloud Favorites · Cute & Kawaii Kids' Books.
- Keyword-target, not interest-target. Pinterest is a search engine wearing a mood board's clothes.

### 5.4 TikTok / Reels / Shorts — *organic first, Spark Ads only on a winner*

No cold paid budget here. Follow `marketing/TIKTOK_LIVE_PLAYBOOK.md` and the 8-format wheel in `GO_VIRAL_PLAN.md` §5. The book-specific formats that work: page-flip ASMR of the reprint art, the "EVERYBODY SQUISH!" shout-along page read aloud, and the dad-story anchor. If any organic video clears ~15k views on its own, put **$50–100 behind it as a Spark Ad** — boosting a proven organic winner is the only paid social that reliably works at this budget.

### 5.5 YouTube — *the free asset we under-use*

The 5:37 read-along already exists and is the strongest single trust asset in the catalog. In Q4:

- Pin it, chapter it, and put the Amazon link plus a "grown-ups: squishysmash.com" card in the description.
- Cut a 60s Shorts version of the shout-along page.
- **Careful:** the read-along is likely "Made for Kids," which disables personalized ads *and* limits how it can be targeted. Any paid YouTube in the All-Out tier must run against **general-audience, parent-facing** creative (the dad story), never against kid-directed content.

### 5.6 Email — the Sparkle Letter

The only owned channel. One dedicated send at the reprint republish, one Picture Book Month send, one "order by Dec 14" send. A permanent one-line P.S. asking for an honest review. Parent-gated always (§9).

## 6. The channels that fund the ads (highest margin in the plan)

Ad royalties are $4.14. **Author copies bought at print cost (~$3.65) and sold in person at $15 are ~$11.35 each** — nearly 3× the margin of an Amazon sale, with zero ad spend.

- **Holiday markets and craft fairs (Nov–Dec, Upstate SC):** 6–10 weekend markets × 12–20 books ≈ **150 books ≈ $1,700 profit.** Booth fees run $25–75. This single line can cover half the Contender budget.
- **Hub City Bookshop + local indie consignment:** typically a 60/40 split; ~$4–5/book, and shelf presence is earned-media bait. `hub_city_event_request_draft.md` is already written.
- **School and library visits (Nov = Picture Book Month):** a read-aloud visit sells 10–40 copies and creates the parent word-of-mouth ads can't buy.
- **Signed copies sold off the website / Sparkle Letter:** full margin minus shipping.

**Do these.** They are less scalable and more work than ads, and they are where a solo author's first thousand books actually go.

## 9. Guardrails (non-negotiable, all channels)

- **No child faces, no real-child voices, no school or location.** The daughters appear only as their character cards plus a first name. Dad films everything.
- **No data capture from kids.** Every email opt-in is parent-gated and headlined to grown-ups.
- **Never imply affiliation with Squishmallows.** Trademark terms are fine as *paid keywords and interest targets*; they must never appear in our listing, backend keywords, A+ content, or ad copy.
- **No incentivized, family, or solicited-positive reviews.** Ever. One honest ask, never chased twice.
- **No production-method claims** ("hand-illustrated" and its cousins) anywhere a buyer can read them.
- **No discounting the paperback below $12.99** during peak. Gift buyers read price as quality, and the royalty has no room.

## 10. Levers considered and deliberately not pulled

- **Hardcover gift edition.** The instinct is right — hardcover is what gift buyers reach for, and gifting is ~42% of picture-book hardcover volume. **But KDP does not offer 8.5×8.5 in hardcover** (hardcover trims are 5.5×8.5, 6×9, 6.14×9.21, 7×10 and 8.25×11 only) — verified against KDP's own trim-size documentation. A square hardcover therefore means IngramSpark, which means buying our own ISBN (the KDP-assigned 979-8199640374 is not portable), a title setup fee, and full-color hardcover print costs of roughly $8–11 on a 40-page square — which at a $21.99 list with a 40% wholesale discount leaves a *thinner* margin than the paperback, not a fatter one. **Verdict: a channel and shelf-presence play, not a margin play.** If you want it for Q4 2027 with bookstore distribution, start in March. Not this quarter. *(Run the real numbers in the IngramSpark calculator before committing — print costs move.)*
- **Kindle edition.** A fixed-layout picture book reads poorly on Kindle and cannibalizes a print gift purchase. Worth doing in January for the new-tablet tail; not worth doing in Q4.
- **BookBub / Freebooksy-style promos.** Built for discounted ebooks; there is no mechanism there for a $12.99 print picture book.
- **Amazon Sponsored Brands / Display.** Requires Brand Registry, which requires a registered trademark. Not available and not worth chasing this quarter — though "Squishy Smash" as a registered mark is a genuinely good 2027 investment for exactly this reason.
- **Discounting into Black Friday.** No room in a $4.14 royalty, and price-cutting a gift item during peak signals the wrong thing.

## 11. Measurement — the four numbers, weekly

Judge trailing data ending **5+ days ago**. The 14-day attribution window means this week's clicks have not finished reporting.

1. **Amazon: blended ACOS and the harvest count.** Months 1–2 tolerate 60% on Exact/PT, 100% on AUTO/Phrase. From Nov 1, ratchet toward 45%.
2. **Meta / Pinterest: cost per outbound click.** Above $0.60 on Meta or $0.40 on Pinterest after 1,000 impressions, the creative is wrong — swap the image, not the audience.
3. **Review count on Book 2.** The compounding number. Every review permanently raises the return on every other dollar.
4. **Total units, all channels** — including markets and consignment. The dashboard number is not the business number.

Log one line per campaign per week in a plain spreadsheet. Week-over-week memory is the whole game.

## 12. Decision points, pre-committed

- **Oct 1:** reviews under 5? → Do not open Meta/Pinterest. Drop to Scout, spend October on reviews, re-decide Nov 1. *(Losing three weeks of Q4 hurts less than buying clicks to a page that can't convert them.)*
- **Nov 1:** Amazon blended ACOS above 120% with 5+ reviews in place? → The problem is the page, not the bids. Pause, fix the cover thumbnail or the description, restart Nov 8.
- **Dec 1:** any channel below half the efficiency of the best channel? → Move its remaining budget to the winner for the peak. Do not be fair to channels.
- **Dec 16:** cut everything to a $5/day always-on floor. Write the retro while the numbers are fresh: which terms, which images, which audience. **That retro is the actual deliverable of this campaign** — it is what Book 3 launches on.

## 13. What "done" looks like on Dec 15

Not a bestseller badge. This:

- **300–500 total units sold** across all channels in the quarter (ads + markets + consignment + direct).
- **15–25 honest reviews** on Book 2, 8+ on Book 1.
- **A one-page search-term map** of the 10–20 queries and 5–10 competitor pages that actually convert this book.
- **A proven creative set** — one winning image, one winning video, one winning headline — that Books 3–6 reuse from day one.
- **A few hundred more homes** with a Squishy Smash book, a QR code, and a magic word in them.

That is an unglamorous, honest list, and it is worth $3,600 in the only quarter where it can be bought.
