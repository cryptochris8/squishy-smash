# Craft Brief: Designing 18 Facing-Pair Spreads for "Squishy Smash: The Lost Sparkle"

*Research output, 2026-05-25. Picture-book craft fundamentals for the 2:1 facing-pair regen.*

A working brief synthesizing current picture-book craft thinking (post-2018 sources where possible), tailored to your 2:1 spread regeneration, 877-word read-aloud arc, shout-line climax, and bedtime-style soft close.

---

## 1. The 2:1 Spread as a Single Composition (Gutter & Eye Flow)

**Treat the gutter as a structural fact, not a center line.** Industry-standard binding loses roughly 0.5 inches at the center; the two cardinal rules are: (a) **never run diagonal or horizontal lines straight across the gutter** (they misalign at bind), and (b) **never place a face, eye, or critical action in the gutter** — enlarge or recompose so the load-bearing element lands at least ~0.75–1 inch off-center.

**Use the gutter as drama, not a wound.** The gutter can mean "wall," "horizon," "between worlds," or "the cut" — Klassen famously lets the unseen happen across it. In *Sam and Dave Dig a Hole*, the gutter functions as the axis the hole descends past, and what one character knows vs. what the reader sees is partitioned by it. Borrow this: when a Squishy is about to discover something, put the discovered object on the *right* page just past the gutter so the page turn (or the eye-jump across the gutter) becomes the reveal.

**Eye-flow defaults for Western readers.** Eyes land top-left, sweep down/right, then exit bottom-right. That makes bottom-right the natural cliffhanger pocket — end the text line or land the "turn-the-page" trigger here. The dominant safe layouts are *text-left / picture-right* and *picture-top / text-bottom*; deviating from these should be a deliberate moment, not a default.

**One composition or two panels?** Reserve a *single spanning composition* for emotional peaks (the shout-line, the resolution), the establishing world, and big scale shifts. Use *implied panels within a spread* (no hard borders — soft vignettes, a tree-line, a wash break) for sequence beats, time compression, or "and then… and then…" moments. Doug Salati does this explicitly in *Hot Dog*: heavy pencil panel borders compress the dog's stressed city scenes, then dissolve to borderless watercolor when he reaches the beach — the *change in framing is the emotional shift*.

## 2. Page-Turn Pacing as Narrative Grammar

**Every page turn is a hinge.** Strong picture books treat the turn as a cliffhanger device — half-revealed art, a clause ending in *"until…"* or *"but…"*, a character mid-leap. Park these triggers at the bottom-right of the verso–recto pair.

**Scale variation = visual rhythm.** Think like a film editor. Across 18 spreads, plan a rhythm of: establishing wide → mid → close-up → wide release → quiet → splash → quiet → splash, etc. A book where every spread is full-bleed splash flattens; one where every spread is the same framed mid-shot dies.

**Suggested arc map for your 18 spreads:**

- **Spreads 1–2 (Opening / world establish):** Wide establishing, full-bleed, generous color saturation. Text-left, image-right.
- **Spreads 3–6 (Rising/journey):** Alternate framed mid-shots and one quiet white-bordered spread.
- **Spreads 7–10 (Stakes rise):** Tighten in. Close-ups of faces; let one Squishy nearly break the gutter on the right page. Begin the palette dimming.
- **Spreads 11–13 (Crisis / dark night):** Lowest light, most negative space, most restraint. Use a **quiet spread before the climax**.
- **Spread 12 — "EVERYBODY SQUISH!" climax:** Full-bleed splash, single spanning composition, type integrated as image, highest chroma in the book.
- **Spreads 13–17 (Resolution):** Borderless watercolor release, warm light returning.
- **Spread 18 (Bedtime close):** Quiet, framed, contained. Echoes spread 1 compositionally — the **circle-back ending**.

**Full-bleed vs. white-bordered is a meaning choice, not a style choice.** Full-bleed = the world swallows the child, immersion, big emotion. White-bordered = a held breath, a memory, a quieter beat.

## 3. Color & Atmosphere Systems

**Build a story-wide palette + per-spread sub-palettes.** Vashti Harrison's *Big* (2024 Caldecott) is the cleanest current example: one anchor color (soft ballet pink) tied to the protagonist, with *saturated* versions at emotional peaks and *desaturated* versions at low points.

**For Squishy Smash:** Pick one Squishy-signature hue (perhaps the "sparkle" itself — a specific candy-bright lavender or mint), and let the loss/return of the sparkle drive a chroma curve across all 18 spreads.
- Spreads 1–6: full saturation
- Spreads 7–11: progressive desaturation as the sparkle goes missing
- Spread 12 (climax): signature hue floods back at maximum saturation
- Spreads 13–18: gently softens toward warm dusk tones for the bedtime close

**Dimming as a bedtime-close technique.** *Goodnight Moon* is the canonical reference: pages literally dim spread over spread, while the moon outside grows brighter — calibrated transition prepares the child physiologically for sleep. Steal for Spreads 16–18.

**"Soft" book atmosphere is achieved through restraint, not softness of brush.** Pick 4–6 colors per spread, lock them, let texture (grain, brush, paper) carry the warmth.

## 4. Character Anchoring Across 18 Spreads

**The silhouette test.** Fill each Squishy in solid black. If a 5-year-old can still tell Soft Dumpling from Goo Ball from Blushy Bun Bunny, you're safe.

**Lock these per-character invariants before generating any spread:**
1. Silhouette shape & head-to-body ratio (kawaii sweet spot: ~1:1 head-to-body, large eyes set low on the face, stubby limbs)
2. Eye shape, position, and proportion (eyes carry the entire emotional vocabulary)
3. Line weight (one weight for outer silhouette; never let this shift between spreads)
4. Highlight/shadow logic (e.g., a single specular dot top-left of each Squishy body, consistent through the book)
5. Scale relative to environment (a reference height chart — never violate)

**Reference-sheet discipline.** Before regeneration, produce a one-page model sheet per Squishy: front, 3/4, side, back, plus 6–8 expression studies. This sheet is the only source of truth for every spread.

**Lighting consistency anchors characters across vastly different settings.** Christopher Denise's *Knight Owl* moves Owl through wildly different settings — Owl reads as the same character because Denise keeps the firelight warm-tone on Owl's chest constant. Apply: pick a hero lighting direction and color for the Squishies; let environments shift but never recompute the hero light.

## 5. Six Books to Study

1. **Big** — Vashti Harrison (2024 Caldecott). Palette-as-character; saturation tells emotional story.
2. **Hot Dog** — Doug Salati (2023 Caldecott). Framing-as-feeling; hard-bordered → borderless watercolor for emotional release.
3. **Knight Owl** — Christopher Denise (2023 Honor). Plush/cute character that reads heroic. Cinematic firelight.
4. **Sam and Dave Dig a Hole** — Klassen (2014 Honor). Gutter as narrative axis; dramatic-irony composition.
5. **The Wolf, the Duck & the Mouse** — Klassen (2017). Eyes as entire emotional vocabulary.
6. **Du Iz Tak?** — Carson Ellis (2016 Honor). Tiny-creature ensemble; fixed-camera staging.

Bonus: **Mel Fell** (2021), **Chooch Helped** (2025), **Hello Lighthouse** (2018).

## 6. Text & Image Interaction

**Body type:** 16–20 pt, line-spacing 1.3–1.5×, sans-serif or warm humanist serif. Good defaults: Adelle, Mrs Eaves, Sentinel, FF Tisa, Brandon Grotesque. Never set body in hand-drawn display.

**Display / shout-line type:** "EVERYBODY SQUISH!" must be **hand-lettered as illustration, integrated into the spread art** — not set in a font. This is the moment to break every other typographic rule.

**Body copy placement rules:**
- Reserve a designed text well in negative space on every spread BEFORE rendering the focal art
- Default placements: top-left of verso, top-right of recto, or centered band across bottom third
- Don't run a single line across the gutter
- Alternate text-page sides across consecutive spreads
- Never embed final body text in the illustration raster — keep it as live type (exception: shout-line + in-world signage)

**Bedtime-close type treatment:** Spreads 16–18: drop type size by 1–2 pt, increase line-spacing, shift black type to a warm dark brown or deep indigo.

---

## TL;DR — Decision Checklist for Each Spread

Before generating any spread, answer:
1. Pacing slot: establish / mid / close-up / quiet / splash / climax / release / close?
2. Frame choice: Full-bleed or white-bordered? Single composition or implied panels?
3. Palette: Which 4–6 colors? Where on the chroma curve?
4. Gutter plan: What's at the center? What crosses it?
5. Eye flow: Where does the eye enter and exit? Page-turn trigger at bottom-right?
6. Character continuity: Do silhouettes match the model sheet?
7. Text well: Where is the ~25–35% quiet area for body type?
8. Climax exception (spread 12 only): Is the shout-line hand-lettered, integrated as composition, at max saturation?
