# Squishy Smash — Story Bible

*The canonical mythology for the Squishy Smash universe. Locked 2026-05-13. This document is the load-bearing lore that every future Squishy Smash storybook, marketing surface, App Store description, and merchandise concept must align with.*

> If something contradicts this bible, the bible wins.

---

## 1. The One-Sentence World Law

> **A squishy is a wish that found a shape. The Squishkeeper sees them, and writes it down, so they stay.**

That is the entire physics of the world. Everything else is a consequence of that sentence.

---

## 2. The Three Canon Lines (drop-in for any asset)

These three phrases are now canonical. They may appear verbatim in any Squishy Smash book, App Store copy, social post, merch, or trailer voiceover:

- **The Pact:** *"Every pop is a hello. Every hello comes back."*
- **The Squishkeeper's job:** *"To see them, and write it down, so they stay."*
- **The Sparkle, defined:** *"The light that comes from being found."*

---

## 3. The Cosmology

| Question | Canonical answer |
|---|---|
| **What ARE squishies?** | Wishes that found a shape. Soft pockets of feeling — comfort, surprise, courage, hush — that the Squishy World grew bodies around so children could hold them. They are made of **wish-stuff**, not matter — which is why they wobble. |
| **Who is the Squishkeeper?** | The first soft thing that ever noticed another soft thing. Not a god. Not a creator. A **witness**. Round, probably. The act of being seen is what lets a squishy stay in the world; the Squishkeeper's job is to keep seeing them, on every page, so they don't blink out. |
| **Why do squishies pop?** | **A pop is a hello, not a goodbye.** When a squishy is squeezed, all the feeling packed inside has to go somewhere — so it bursts outward as color, sound, and a tiny shockwave of joy. The squishy reforms in the great soft elsewhere, ready to come back. **The game is the finding; the book is the catching.** |
| **What unifies and distinguishes the three packs?** | All three packs are made of the same wish-stuff, but each formed around a different **first feeling** — Pudding Hills around *comfort* (warm + sweet), Goo Coast around *surprise* (glossy + bouncy), Moonlit Hollow around *brave-cuddle* (the feeling of a child meeting the dark and finding it soft). |

### Canonical terminology

- **Wish-stuff** is the word for what squishies are made of. Always lowercase, hyphenated. Not "sparkle-stuff," not "soft-stuff," not "essence." Wish-stuff.
- **The great soft elsewhere** is where squishies go between pops. Use sparingly — it is a phrase, not a place name. Do not name or map the elsewhere.
- **The Sparkle** is the shared light of being-seen. Capital S when referring to the cosmic mechanic; lowercase when referring to incidental sparkle on a character (e.g., Sparkle Mochi's glitter).

## 4. The Founding Myth — "The First Wobble"

The canonical creation sequence. Reference these beats in any origin retelling.

1. **Before.** The Squishy World was perfectly still. Nothing had ever moved, because nothing had ever been *felt*.
2. **The First Wobble.** A single small thing — a wish so small no one remembers whose it was — rolled across the stillness. It left a *pmf.* The Squishkeeper, who was already round and already paying attention, saw it. **That seeing made it real.**
3. **The Three Spillings.** The first wobble was too full of feeling to stay one shape, so it spilled in three directions:
   - Where it spilled *warm*, the **Pudding Hills** rose — syrup pooled, sprinkles fell as snow.
   - Where it spilled *glossy*, the **Goo Coast** stretched out — the tide learned to *sploink*.
   - Where it spilled *quiet*, **Moonlit Hollow** unfolded — under a moon that turned itself down to nightlight-soft.
4. **The Three Cores.** Each spilling left a single drop of *original* wish-stuff behind. Those drops became the three Legendaries:
   - **Celestial Dumpling Core** — the warm drop. Taught the stars to glow by example.
   - **Singularity Goo Core** — the glossy drop. So dense with feeling that gravity bends to look at it.
   - **Mythic Plush Familiar** — the quiet drop. Walks the edge of every Hollow making sure no squishy gets lost in its own shadow.
5. **The Forty-Five Followers.** Once the Cores existed, smaller wishes had somewhere to belong. Forty-five more squishies wobbled into being — sixteen per region minus the Core. Each one a feeling a child somewhere needed a soft body for. The Squishkeeper began the book.
6. **The Pact of the Pop.** The Cores agreed: a squishy that is never squeezed is a feeling that is never shared. So they promised — *every pop returns. Every squish is a hello.* The book records the finding; the world handles the reforming.

## 5. Rarity-as-Lore

Rarity is **how much wish-stuff a squishy is made of**, not how hard it is to find. This makes the game's gacha hierarchy meaningful in-world.

| Tier | In-world meaning | Per pack | Examples |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Common (★)** | *Everyday feelings.* The ones a child meets most often: soft, glossy, hoppy. Pop quickly, reform quickly. Pack-mates because everyday feelings travel in pairs (comfort+curiosity, stretch+squeeze, hop+hush). | 8 | Soft Dumpling, Goo Ball, Blushy Bun Bunny |
| **Rare (★★)** | *Feelings with weather inside them.* Carry a tiny atmospheric event — glitter on the floor, slow-motion splat, a wish behind each eye. A Rare's pop briefly changes the **air** around it. | 4 | Sparkle Mochi, Glitter Goo Ball, Star-Eyed Bunny |
| **Epic (★★★)** | *Feelings borrowed from the sky.* The moment a child first realizes the world is *bigger* than the room. An Epic's pop changes the **room**. | 3 | Galaxy Dumpling, Aurora Stretch Cube, Glow Ghost Puff |
| **Legendary / Mythic (★★★★)** | *Original wish-stuff.* The three Cores. They don't really pop in the game sense; they **bloom**. Their job is structural — they hold their pack-world together, and when the Squishkeeper is asleep, they keep the seeing going. | 1 | Celestial Dumpling Core, Singularity Goo Core, Mythic Plush Familiar |

**Narrative consequence:** a Common is not lesser — it is *closer to the child*. The Cores are farther away precisely because they are larger. Book 2's "Common saves the day" subversion is **mythologically load-bearing**: small feelings can carry the world because they are the ones children actually hold.

## 6. The Voice

**Tonal lane: Adventurous (Pokémon-mythic) with a soft landing.**

Every Squishy Smash book opens with scope, wonder, and journey energy — and closes intimate, cozy, repeatable. The arc happens both across the series AND within each book.

### The Squishkeeper's voice

- **A chronicler-traveler.** Has walked every shore and counted every cloud, and is still not done. Keeps the map. Keeps the names.
- **Still tender.** Adventurous does not mean breezy. The Squishkeeper is awed by what they record.
- **Direct address.** Speaks to "you," the reader, often. ("I have been saving this one for you.")
- **Italic only when the Squishkeeper is speaking.** Body prose stays roman.
- **Periods, not em dashes.** Sentence shapes vary — short, then short, then a longer one that rolls a little — but the punctuation stays plain.
- **Mystery is a feature.** The Squishkeeper does not explain themselves. ("I am, I think, a little bit round.")

### The within-book arc

Every book moves from **scope → intimacy**.

- **Opening pages:** wonder, scale, the world is big. *("From that first wobble, three places woke up...")*
- **Middle:** the journey or quest itself. Adventure tone dominant.
- **Climax:** the Big Pop — maximum color, maximum sound, the call-and-response moment.
- **Final spread:** quiet, cozy, repeatable. The kind of line parents say on autopilot by month three. *("And somewhere, another squishy is waiting. Tap it. Squish it. Pop.")*

The soft landing is what makes the book survive read #47. Don't skip it.

### Reference sample line (the Voice in one paragraph)

> *"The pop is how a squishy goes home. A flash of color, a happy sound, a burst of goo — and away. Back to wherever squishies come from. Back to the great soft elsewhere. Then, when you're ready: another wobble. Another adventure. They always come back."*

## 7. Visual Rules

### The Squishkeeper is **never drawn**

- No silhouette. No outline. No "implied figure in the background." Never.
- The Squishkeeper exists only in **text** — narrator voice on the page, never as a character on the page.
- This is a hard rule across all books, all merch, all marketing. The mystery is the franchise fuel; one drawing collapses it forever.
- Acceptable visual stand-ins: a quill, a half-open journal, a faint pawprint, a single round shadow. None of these are *the* Squishkeeper — they are objects the Squishkeeper has touched.

### Everything else from book #1 carries forward

- Brand palette: `#120B17` deep starry-night base, brand pink, cream, jelly-blue / lavender / lime pack accents
- Display font Fredoka; body EB Garamond; accent Caveat Brush
- Pack ornaments in corners (sprinkles / bubbles / moondust)
- Visual consistency across templates — same primitives, applied uniformly

## 8. Series Scaffolding

The Sparkle = the shared light of being-seen, fueled by squishies popping and the Squishkeeper recording. When pops stop returning, the Sparkle dims. **This single mechanic powers all later books.**

| Book | Title (working) | Shape of question | Hero focus |
|---|---|---|---|
| **2** | *The Lost Sparkle* | What happens when the cycle of pop-and-return breaks? | Trio of Commons (Soft Dumpling, Goo Ball, Blushy Bun Bunny) cross pack borders for the first time |
| **3** | *Pudding Hills* | "What does comfort do when the world gets cold?" | Galaxy Dumpling — comfort sent across distances |
| **4** | *Goo Coast* | "What does surprise do when nothing changes?" | Aurora Stretch Cube — the tide that forgot how to come in |
| **5** | *Moonlit Hollow* | "What does the dark do when no one is afraid of it?" | Mythic Plush Familiar walks a child home through Whisper Woods |
| **6** | *The Three Cores* | "What is the Squishkeeper, really?" | The only book that comes close to answering — and answers *just* far enough that the mystery survives |

### Deliberate incompleteness rule

- Pokémon waited 8 years before naming a creator god. Ghibli never explains Totoro. **Squishy Smash follows this model.**
- Book 6 approaches the Squishkeeper answer but does not collapse it. The mystery survives.
- Do not introduce a villain. Tension comes from *situation* (Sparkle dimming, tide forgetting, dark feeling lonely), never opposition. Sylvanian Families built a 40-year franchise with no antagonist — the same rule applies here.

## 9. The Pop, Operationally

How "the pop" works across surfaces:

| Surface | What "the pop" looks like |
|---|---|
| **iOS game** | The current burst — color, sound, particles, screen shake. Per the Pact, the squishy *returns* in a later round. Frame in marketing as "they always come back," never as defeat. |
| **Book #1** (in production) | Implicit. Squishkeeper records each squishy. Pop is not depicted; the bios are timeless. |
| **Book #2** (*The Lost Sparkle*) | **The Pop is the engine of the plot.** The Sparkle dims when pops stop returning — this is the inciting problem the trio must fix. The Pact line ("Every pop is a hello. Every hello comes back.") should appear at least once, near the climax. |
| **Marketing / App Store** | The Pact line is brand-safe and parent-friendly. Reframes the game from "smash" → "find, squish, return." Especially useful when targeting parent purchasers who pause at the word "smash." |
| **Merchandise** | The Pact line is the printable hook. T-shirt, sticker, plush hangtag. Three lines that can carry the whole brand without depicting the Squishkeeper. |

## 10. Architectures Considered + Rejected

(For future reference — when a new collaborator suggests one of these, point them here.)

| # | Alternative | Why rejected |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Squishies are **toys come to life** (Velveteen Rabbit / Toy Story lineage) | Too derivative; collapses the game-vs-book tension instead of resolving it (a smashed toy is still tragic) |
| 2 | Squishies are **aliens / a fallen civilization** (Cores as crashed ships) | Wrong tonal lane for ages 4–8; introduces threat into a universe whose promise is *safe* |
| 3 | Squishies are **dreams the Squishkeeper is having** | Pretty, but makes the game existentially weird (player intrudes on someone's sleep). "It was all a dream" closes the series door |
| 4 | Squishies are **literal food / objects** (Pudding Hills is a real bakery) | Game ↔ book tension gets worse (smashing a dumpling is eating; smashing a bunny is not). Wish-stuff fixes this: Pudding Hills is shaped *like* warm food because comfort *feels* like warm food, not because it's literally edible |

## 11. Research-validated precedents

- **Pop = teleport, not death** is officially the **Kirby** model. HAL Laboratory director Shinya Kumazaki confirmed: enemies are teleported elsewhere in the world. Direct precedent for our Pact. ([source](https://www.nintendolife.com/news/2023/03/random-its-okay-everyone-kirby-doesnt-digest-his-enemies))
- **Place over cosmology** (Beatrix Potter, Sylvanian Families) — durable for ages 4–8. Pudding Hills / Goo Coast / Moonlit Hollow follows this.
- **Deliberate incompleteness** (Pokémon waited 8 years; Ghibli never explains Totoro). Squishkeeper's mystery is canonical and load-bearing.
- **No villain required** — Sylvanian Families' 40-year run with zero antagonists. Tension comes from situation, not opposition.
- **Creature taxonomy as series engine** (Hilda's elves/trolls/Marra; Pokémon's 1000+ species). Our 48-character roster IS the mythology.
