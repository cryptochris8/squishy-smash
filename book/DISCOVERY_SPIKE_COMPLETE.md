## Step 3 — Parent-problem articulation

**Book #1 is a reference. Book #2 is a ritual.**

A parent opens *Meet the Squishies* when their child asks "who is this one?" — and closes it once that question is answered. It is wonderful for the morning rug. It is useless at 8:15 PM. *The Lost Sparkle* is the bedtime story Book #1 cannot be: a single 18-spread arc with a Big Pop the child can shout along to (the "EVERYBODY SQUISH!" call-and-response moment), and a final line the parent says on autopilot by month three. The problem Book #2 solves is the one Book #1 creates — once your kid is in love with these 48 characters, you need a way to put them to bed.

That answer does **not** collapse to "more characters" (it has the same 48). It does **not** collapse to "more art" (the value is in the arc and the ritual, not asset count). It's plain English, repeatable on a back-cover blurb, and the same answer holds for a parent, a bookseller, or an Amazon shopper skimming reviews.

**Verdict: Step 3 PASS.**
